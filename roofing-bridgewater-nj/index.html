Local Conditions

Common roofing challenges in Bridgewater, NJ

The problems Bridgewater homeowners ask us about most are storm water, older patched roofs, wind on hillside lots, chimney and skylight leaks, and gutters that overflow onto the fascia. A township this large has many kinds of house, so the trouble on the roof varies by street.

  • Storm and Flood-Season Water Hurricane Irene flooded the old American Cyanamid site on the Raritan in August 2011, and heavy rain still tests every roof in the valley. We check valleys, step flashing and drip edge, because that is where driven rain gets in.
  • Older Roofs in Established Neighborhoods Bradley Gardens and the older streets near Foothill Road in Finderne have long-standing houses. A roof that has been patched several times usually needs the deck checked before any new shingles go on.
  • Wind on Hillside Lots Martinsville's hilly ground leaves some roofs more exposed than a flat lot. Lifted shingle tabs and creased ridge caps are common after a blow, and we replace them on the spot or flag them for a larger repair.
  • Chimney and Skylight Leaks Larger homes often have more chimneys, skylights and dormers, which means more flashing that can fail. Fresh flashing at each one is part of a proper replacement.
  • Gutters That Overflow Onto the Fascia When seamless gutters are undersized or pitched wrong, water spills behind them and rots the fascia. We install seamless aluminum gutters and set the pitch toward the downspouts.

If one of these sounds like your house, we will come and look, take the photos, and tell you what we see.

About the Area

All about Bridgewater, NJ

Bridgewater received its royal charter on April 4, 1749 and was incorporated in 1798, then lost land over the next century to Warren, Branchburg, Somerville, Raritan and Bound Brook. What is left is 32.36 square miles, with 16,657 housing units counted in 2010.

A township that size is really several communities, and a roofer who treats it as one place misses the details. Martinsville, Green Knoll, Finderne, Bradley Gardens and the Milltown and Vanderveer Road area each have a different mix of houses.

The water matters to a roof here. The North and South Branches of the Raritan meet at the confluence in the township's southwest corner, and the river runs along the southern border. When Hurricane Irene came through in August 2011, floodwater covered the old American Cyanamid site beside the Raritan, which gives a sense of how much rain this stretch of the valley can collect.

More about roofing in Bridgewater

Roofs do not flood, but saturated ground, clogged gutters and wind-driven rain all show up in the attic. We look for the water stains and soft decking that heavy storms leave behind.

The history is close to the surface too. The Van Veghten House, dating to about 1725, and the Van Horne House, from about 1750, are both on the National Register, and the Middlebrook encampment of 1778 and 1779 is remembered at Washington Camp Ground. Few homes here are that old, but it is a reminder that Bridgewater has been settled for a long time and some houses have had several roofs.

We do not guess how many layers you have. We open the edge, count, and show you.

Our owner drives out from the Somerset shop for the inspection himself. Whether the house is a brick colonial in Finderne or a newer build near Bridgewater Commons, the process is the same. We tear off to the deck, replace any damaged decking, lay ice and water shield in the valleys and eaves, add synthetic underlayment and drip edge, set ridge ventilation, and flash every penetration.

Full replacements carry our 25-year workmanship warranty. We finish with a magnetic nail sweep across your yard and driveway and a walkthrough with photos.

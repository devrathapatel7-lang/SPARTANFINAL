The Difference

Why Somerset County homeowners choose Spartan Roofing for skylight installation

  • Framed into the roof structure

    The opening is cut and framed to the rafters, not just trimmed through the sheathing. We look at the attic first to see where the rafters run, so the skylight sits where the structure allows and where you want the light.

  • Flashing built around the opening

    Most skylight leaks are flashing leaks. The flashing is layered with the shingles and the underlayment, and ice and water shield goes around the opening, so water runs over each layer instead of behind it.

  • Done with your roof, not against it

    A skylight on a roof that is nearing replacement is a poor bet. We tell you honestly if the shingles around the spot are close to the end, and we can set the skylight in as part of a replacement.

What a skylight installation in Somerset County, NJ involves

Putting a skylight into a roof is mostly a waterproofing job. A roof sheds water because every layer overlaps the one below it, so when you cut a hole through those layers you have to rebuild the overlap on all four sides of the opening.

The upper side needs to divert water around the unit. The lower side needs to let it run out over the shingles.

More about skylight installation

The sides need to step with each course. The flashing does all three.

A recent job, from a Google review by Martin Joyce: a skylight at his mother's home was leaking. Our owner diagnosed it in minutes as a defective skylight and sent a quote. Once it was accepted, our crew came within days and installed the new skylight within hours.

We start in the attic, where we find the rafters, check the insulation and the ceiling below, and decide where the unit can sit. Then we look at the roof from outside, where the shingles stand, where the ridge and any vents are, and how the slope will carry water toward the skylight.

Placement matters for light and for water. A skylight set just below a ridge line sheds water easily, while one at the bottom of a long, wide slope has a lot of roof running water toward it.

Fixed skylights do not open and are the simplest, while opening models let air through.

Some units come with options such as solar operation or room-darkening shades. We go over the choices at the estimate, including which size fits between the rafters without extra framing, and write down the one you pick.

If the roof around the spot is old, the better time to put in a skylight is during a replacement, so the flashing goes in with new underlayment and new shingles. When the roof has years left, we can install on the existing shingles, tying the new flashing into what is there.

Replacing an old skylight is a different job. Sometimes the unit itself has failed, with a cracked seal or a fogged pane, and sometimes the glass is fine and the flashing is what let go.

We find out which before we quote. A homeowner who calls about a drip often does not need a new unit, and we say so.

The light has to travel from the roof opening down through the attic space into the room, and how that is finished depends on your house. We walk you through the ceiling-side work at the estimate so nothing about it is a surprise.

When the unit is in, we take photos of the flashing and the finished roof and walk the job with you. Manufacturer coverage applies to the skylight and to the materials. Our 25-year workmanship warranty applies to full roof replacements, so if you are doing a skylight together with a new roof, ask us how that is written up.

Watch For These

Signs your Somerset County home needs skylight installation

  • Drips, stains or a damp ceiling around an existing skylight after rain
  • Condensation or fog trapped between the panes of the glass
  • Cracked or yellowed glass or acrylic, or a frame that has warped
  • Flashing around the unit that is lifting, cracked or sealed with a thick bead of caulk
  • A dark room or hallway where you have wanted daylight without adding a window wall
  • A roof being replaced where an old skylight sits in the middle of it

FAQs

Skylight installation in Somerset County, NJ: frequently asked questions

Can a skylight be installed on an existing roof in Somerset, NJ?

Yes, if the shingles around the spot have years left, we install the skylight and flash it into what is there. If the roof is close to replacement, we will tell you it is smarter to do the skylight with the new roof.

Do skylights leak, and how do you keep them dry in Somerset County?

A skylight leaks when its flashing is wrong or has aged out. To keep the odds low, we frame the opening to the rafters, put ice and water shield around it and layer the flashing with the shingles.

Can you replace a leaking skylight in Somerset County?

Yes, once we work out whether the unit has failed or only the flashing has. If the glass is fine, new flashing can be a smaller job than a new skylight.

Do I need a permit for a skylight in Somerset, NJ?

The town construction office issues permits, and we handle the paperwork if one is needed for your job. Ask us at the estimate.

Do you install skylights as part of a new roof in Somerset County, NJ?

Yes, and it is the cleanest way to do it. The flashing goes in with new underlayment and new shingles, so there is no seam between old and new work around the opening.

What warranty covers a new skylight from Spartan Roofing in Somerset County, NJ?

Skylights and roofing materials carry the manufacturer's coverage. Our 25-year workmanship warranty applies to full roof replacements. For a skylight-only job, ask at the estimate and we will put the terms in writing.

Is the skylight inspection free in Somerset, NJ?

Yes, with no pressure. We check the attic and the roof, talk through where the skylight can go and what it needs, and give you a written estimate. Call (973) 938-2776 to set a time.

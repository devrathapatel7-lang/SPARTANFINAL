Choosing and installing siding that holds up in Somerset County

Siding that holds up keeps rain out of the wall behind it, so we help you choose vinyl or James Hardie fiber cement and install it for that first. Siding does two jobs: it gives the house its look, and it sheds rain so the wall behind it stays dry. Most homeowners compare the first job and forget the second.

In Somerset, NJ a house sees wind-driven rain, a hard freeze every winter, and summer sun on the south and west walls. Siding that looks good but lets water get behind it can rot sheathing for years before anyone notices. Everything we do on a siding job is aimed at that second job, because a wall that stays dry lasts and a wall that does not has to be opened up again.

More about siding installation

A recent job, from a Google review in June 2026: Matt Karp's house is more than 100 years old. We installed new roof soffits and attic ventilation, sent him status updates along the way and pointed out other things to consider on the house.

Vinyl is lightweight, comes in a wide range of colors and profiles, and the color runs through the panel instead of sitting on the surface. It expands and contracts with temperature, so it is nailed loose in its slots, not driven tight, and that detail separates an installation that ripples from one that lies flat. James Hardie fiber cement is a heavy, rigid, cement-based board that is cut and handled differently, holds paint and a crisp shadow line well, and gives a house a look closer to painted wood.

Neither is wrong. We price both for your house on the same estimate, and you choose with real numbers in front of you.

The work starts with the wall. We remove the old siding and look at the sheathing and the framing underneath. If the wall behind it is wet or soft, we show you and tell you what has to be corrected before new siding goes on.

House wrap goes over the sheathing and is lapped like shingles, so water that gets past the siding runs down and out. Windows, doors and corners get flashing and sealing where they need it. Insulation board over the sheathing is an option for some walls, and we will tell you at the walkthrough if it makes sense for yours.

A roofing crew brings one real advantage to siding. Roof-to-wall joints, dormers, porch roofs and chimney chases are where a wall and a roof meet, and those spots leak when the siding and the roofing were done by people who never talked to each other. We install step flashing at those joints and cut the siding to sit over it.

We also finish soffit, fascia and J-channel so the trim lines match the new siding and the whole exterior reads as one job. If your roof is due for work as well, planning both together means the flashing and the siding are tied together properly.

Preparation also protects your property. Shrubs and walkways are covered before tear-off, old siding is hauled away, and we sweep the yard and driveway with a magnetic roller for nails at the end. Siding materials carry their own manufacturer-backed warranties, and we register what can be registered and write the scope into your estimate.

Our 25-year workmanship warranty is for full roof replacements, so ask how workmanship terms apply to a siding job and we will answer in writing. Ask about the $500 discount and low-rate financing when your estimate comes in; the terms depend on the job and we do not guess at them.

Watch For These

Signs your Somerset County home needs siding installation

  • Cracked, warped or buckled panels, especially on the sunny sides of the house
  • Siding that has faded, chalked or lost its color
  • Boards that feel soft or look swollen near the ground or under windows
  • Gaps or loose panels that rattle in the wind
  • Water stains or peeling paint on the inside wall under a window
  • Mildew or dark streaks that come back after washing
  • Missing or damaged soffit, fascia or J-channel along the roof edge

FAQs

Siding installation in Somerset County, NJ: frequently asked questions

Should I choose vinyl or James Hardie siding for my home in Somerset County?

It depends on the house, the look you want and the budget. Vinyl is lighter, colored all the way through and easier to install over a wide range of walls. Fiber cement is a denser board that holds paint and sharp lines and suits homeowners who want a painted-wood look. We price both for your house on one estimate and explain the tradeoffs so the choice is yours.

Do you remove the old siding before installing new siding in Somerset, NJ?

Yes. Old siding comes off, we inspect the sheathing and framing, and we tell you about anything that needs correcting before new material goes up. Covering over old siding hides problems, so tear-off is how we start.

Is house wrap really necessary under new siding in Somerset?

Yes. Siding sheds most rain, but wind pushes some water behind it. House wrap is the second layer that catches it and sends it back out. We install it on every siding job and flash windows, doors and roof joints over it.

Can you replace soffit and fascia along with siding in Somerset County?

Yes. Soffit, fascia and J-channel are part of our siding scope, and we match them to the new siding. Those parts sit at the roof edge, where a roofing crew is already working.

Does new siding in Somerset need a permit?

The township construction office issues permits for exterior work in Somerset, and what is required depends on the project. We handle the paperwork for you.

What warranty comes with siding installed by Spartan Roofing?

Vinyl and James Hardie fiber cement both carry manufacturer-backed material warranties, and we register what can be registered. Workmanship terms go into your written estimate. Our 25-year workmanship warranty is for full roof replacements, so ask how terms apply to siding.

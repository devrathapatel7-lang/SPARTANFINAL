Local Conditions

Common roofing challenges in Hillsborough, NJ

Out here the roof takes the weather without much shelter. These are the problems we hear about most from Hillsborough callers.

  • Leaks That Keep Coming Back A roof that has been repaired by several companies and still leaks usually has a problem the patches did not reach. We trace the water from the ceiling back to the source before recommending anything.
  • Wind Across Open Fields Homes next to farmland and preserves have little cover. Wind lifts shingle edges, creases ridge caps and works under loose flashing.
  • Ice at Cold-Snap Eaves With January lows near 18 degrees on average, meltwater freezes at the roof edge. Ice and water shield at the eaves stops it backing up under the shingles.
  • Oak Debris in Valleys and Gutters Hillsborough sits in what was historically oak forest. If oaks stand over your roofline, branches scrape shingles and leaves and acorns fill the valleys.
  • Low-Slope Sections on Larger Buildings Churches, halls and additions often combine steep and flat roofs. The seam where they meet needs special flashing, and we check it on every inspection.

Tell us about the leak on the phone, and we will come out, climb up and show you what is there.

About the Area

All about Hillsborough, NJ

Hillsborough covers 55.10 square miles, with 43,276 residents in 2020 and 14,030 housing units counted in 2010. Its charter dates to May 31, 1771, and it was incorporated in 1798.

Manville broke away in 1929, and what remains is a township of villages: Belle Mead, Neshanic, Flagtown, Amwell, Woods Tavern and others. About a third of the land is preserved open space or farmland, so the roofs are often spread out, with long driveways and lots of sky around them.

Duke Farms covers around 2,700 acres in the northeast part of the township and opened to the public on May 19, 2012. Sourland Mountain Preserve is nearby.

Local Conditions

Common roofing challenges in Somerset, NJ

The problems we find most on Somerset roofs are attic heat, ice at the eaves, leaks at valleys on tight lots, tired flashing at chimneys and vents, and the joints at additions. Somerset is a dense, mostly built-out section of the township, with more than 9,000 homes packed into roughly six square miles.

  • Attic Heat in Humid Summers Hot, humid summers push attic temperatures high. Where ridge or soffit ventilation is blocked or missing, shingles age early and the decking underneath can stay damp.
  • Ice at the Eaves Winters here are cold enough to freeze meltwater at the edge of the roof. Without ice and water shield along the eaves, that water backs up under the shingles and stains the ceiling below.
  • Tight Lots, Short Roof Runs Houses built close together mean valleys and dormer joints take a lot of water in one spot. Worn valley metal and cracked sealant show up as leaks near the same few seams.
  • Tired Flashing at Chimneys and Vents On a roof that is otherwise sound, the leak is usually where a chimney, plumbing vent or skylight meets the shingles. We replace the flashing instead of smearing sealant over it.
  • Additions and Flat Sections Over a roof's life, many owners add a rear extension or a low-slope section. The joint between old and new roof is a common failure point, and we check it on every inspection.

If a ceiling stain or a missing shingle has you looking up, call the shop and we will book the inspection.

On a Somerset house we fly a drone over the roof for photos, then a crew member walks it and checks flashing, valleys, vents and the condition of the shingles.

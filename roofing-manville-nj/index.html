Local Conditions

Common roofing challenges in Manville, NJ

Manville gets river-valley weather: heavy tropical rain, snowmelt and nor'easters. These are the roof problems that follow.

  • Wind-driven rain after a big storm Hurricane Irene in 2011 and Ida in 2021 both brought heavy rain, and a nor'easter hit in April 2007. Lifted shingles and loose flashing let that rain into the attic.
  • Damp attics After a wet season the underside of the roof deck can stay damp when there is no clear ventilation path, and the wood and insulation suffer. We add ridge ventilation and check the baffles.
  • Fallen limbs and storm debris Sandy in 2012 did not flood the town, but its winds left many homes without power for days. Branches hit roofs in that kind of wind, and we repair or replace the damaged section.
  • Roofs at the end of their life A roof that is past its years leaks first at valleys and around pipes, and a storm only speeds it up. The free inspection shows which spots are failing.
  • Water behind siding and fascia Gutter overflow wets the fascia and the siding below. We install seamless aluminum gutters and replace soffit and fascia where the wood has rotted.

After any storm, call for a free inspection before the next one arrives. We tarp what needs it and show you the photos.

About the Area

All about Manville, NJ

Manville was incorporated in 1929 and takes its name from the Johns-Manville Corporation, which ran a large plant in the borough for decades. The borough covers about two and a half square miles in the Raritan Valley, at around 56 feet above sea level, with Bridgewater, Franklin and Hillsborough townships on its borders.

The Raritan and Millstone rivers join in the northeast corner. Main Street runs along CR 533, and the borough has no interstate, US route or state highway of its own.

Flooding is part of the history here. Hurricane Floyd in September 1999 dropped 10 to 12 inches of rain, and the water in town reached 12 to 17 feet; the town calls it the worst ever to hit Manville, with rescues made by boat or helicopter. Tropical Storm Doria flooded the Lost Valley section in 1971.

More about roofing in Manville

The April 2007 nor'easter and Irene in 2011 brought more. On September 1, 2021, Ida flooded most of the borough, and the Raritan reached 27.66 feet at its gauge, above Floyd's 27.1 feet.

Lost Valley, the eastern section on the flood plain between the two rivers, has taken the worst of it. A roofer does not fix a flood, and we do not claim to.

What we do is the part above the waterline: after a storm we check the roof, the flashing, the attic and the gutters, tarp what is open, and document damage for your insurance company. If your insurer or your flood repair contractor needs a roof report, we put one together with photos.

The borough had about 4,290 housing units at the 2020 count, and most blocks are residential, with Polish-oriented businesses along Main Street.

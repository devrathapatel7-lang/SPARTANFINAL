Why chimneys in Somerset County leak, and what a lasting chimney repair looks like

A chimney is a brick column poking up through a sloped roof, and the two move, shed water and age at different rates. The flashing is the metal that bridges them and keeps that joint watertight.

When it is installed in layers, it handles all of that. When it is a bead of tar or caulk along the brick, it works until the first hard season and then splits. Most chimney leaks we are called to in Somerset, NJ come down to the same story: the original flashing was thin, short on layers, or patched over so many times that the patch became the problem.

More about chimney repair

A recent job, from a Google review in March 2026: Lindsey Borreros called us about a chimney leak. She wrote that we responded fast and quickly addressed the issues with the leak.

Step flashing runs up the sides of the chimney, with each small piece woven under a course of shingles and turned up against the brick. Counter flashing sits over the top of the step pieces, cut or set into the mortar joint so water running down the brick lands on metal and not behind it.

An apron covers the low side, and behind a wide chimney a cricket, a small peaked saddle, splits the water that would otherwise pool uphill. Each piece sheds water onto the next, and that overlap is what keeps the roof dry.

Leaks at a chimney are easy to misread, because water can enter at the flashing, run along the decking and drip from a ceiling in the next room.

That is why we go into the attic first when it is accessible, then up on the roof to match what we saw inside with what is on the outside, taking photos at each step. If the water gets in somewhere else, such as a vent boot or a valley, we say so and fix that instead.

Weather in Somerset County is hard on this joint. Freeze and thaw works water into hairline gaps. Wind-driven rain from a nor'easter pushes sideways against the chimney, which is exactly where an old caulk line fails.

Summer heat expands the metal and the brick by different amounts, so over a few seasons a flashing that was tight when it went in opens up. If a leak only shows up in a heavy, windy storm, flashing is one of the first places we look.

When the chimney needs it, we also repair or rebuild the crown at the top, tuckpoint mortar joints that have opened, install a cap, and seal the masonry so water does not soak through the brick. We do not sweep chimneys.

When we replace a whole roof, new flashing at the chimney is part of the scope, so it is never reused or skipped. Watch For These

Signs your Somerset County home needs chimney repair

  • A water stain on the ceiling or wall next to the chimney
  • Damp insulation or dark staining on the framing around the chimney in the attic
  • Rusted, bent or lifted metal where the chimney meets the roof
  • Black tar or caulk smeared along the edge of the brick
  • A leak that only appears in wind-driven rain
  • Shingles curling or missing right beside the chimney
  • A gap between the flashing and the brick that you can see from the ground

Our Chimney Repair Process

How chimney repair in Somerset County, NJ works with us

We trace the leak, open the joint around the chimney, rebuild it in layers, then seal it and walk the finished work with you. You see photos at each step, with the scope in writing before we start.

  1. Step : 01

    Call, then trace the leak

    Call (973) 938-2776 or send the form and we set a time. We check the attic around the chimney, then go up on the roof and inspect the flashing, the shingles beside it and the joint with the brick. You get photos and a clear answer on where the water gets in.

  2. Step : 02

    Open the joint

    Old caulk, failed metal and damaged shingles around the chimney come out. We check the decking underneath, and any wet or soft wood is replaced before new metal goes on.

  3. Step : 03

    Rebuild in layers

    Crown repair and tuckpointing are done first where the masonry needs them. Then ice and water shield, step flashing, an apron, counter flashing and a cricket where the chimney is wide enough to need one.

  4. Step : 04

    Seal and walk through

    The joints are sealed, the shingles are tied back in, and we run a magnetic nail sweep around the house. We walk the finished work with you and send photos.

FAQs

Chimney repair in Somerset County, NJ: frequently asked questions

Why is my chimney still leaking in Somerset County after it was caulked?

Caulk is a surface seal, and chimneys move, so after a season or two the bead shrinks, cracks and pulls from the brick. A lasting fix replaces the metal in layers, with step flashing under the shingles and counter flashing over it, so the water is shed by overlap and sealant is only a backup.

Can chimney flashing be replaced without replacing the whole roof in Somerset, NJ?

Yes, if the shingles around the chimney are in decent shape, we remove the old flashing, replace the shingles beside the chimney, rebuild the flashing and tie it back into the existing roof. If the roof is near the end of its life, we tell you that at the inspection so you can plan.

Do you repair the chimney crown and mortar near Somerset?

Yes, we repair chimney crowns, tuckpoint mortar joints, install caps and apply a waterproof masonry sealant as part of a chimney repair. We do not sweep chimneys.

How do I know the leak is the flashing and not the chimney itself in Somerset County?

We start in the attic, where stains and wet wood along the sides and base of the chimney point to the flashing, while stains lower on the brick or inside the flue point somewhere else. We then match what we saw inside with the roof outside, which keeps us from repairing the wrong spot.

Does chimney repair in Somerset need a permit?

The township construction office issues permits for roofing work in Somerset. We handle the paperwork for you.

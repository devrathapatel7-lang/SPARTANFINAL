Flemington, NJ Historic Roof Replacement

This guide sets out the roof replacement rules for houses in the Flemington Historic District, taken from Flemington Borough's own Historic Preservation Commission documents. Spartan Roofing is a roofing company based in Somerset, NJ. Our roof replacement page explains how a full replacement works, and our Flemington roofing page covers the borough's roofs in general.

Which roof jobs need a Historic Preservation Commission review in Flemington?

A roof replacement that changes the roof's material or look needs a review before the work starts. The commission's application instructions say any owner planning exterior work on a historic property in the district "that results in a change in material or the look of the property must undergo a Commission Review," and they list roofs and gutters among the work this covers.

The borough's review process page says the same thing from the other side. Apart from maintenance, all exterior work on a property in the Historic District or on a designated landmark needs a review, and roof replacement is one of the items that usually falls under it. Ordinary repair or maintenance in kind does not, and neither does interior work that leaves the exterior alone.

If you can't tell which side of that line your job falls on, the review process page tells owners to contact the Borough Building Department in the earliest planning stages.

How do you know if your house is in Flemington's historic district?

Look your property up on the borough's Searchable Historic District list. The commission's instructions point owners to that list both to confirm a property is in the district and to find its District Category, which the application asks for.

More than half the borough's parcels sit inside the district, 54% by the commission's count, so there is a fair chance your house does. The commission's page also says every property in the district is subject to review, including buildings that are not historic themselves.

The borough's guidelines sort buildings into Key, Contributing and Non-Contributing categories. Have your block and lot number ready as well; the instructions say it is printed on your tax assessment or tax bill.

What goes into a roof application?

A complete application has five parts, and the commission returns applications that are missing details. According to the commission's application instructions, you need:

  • The application form, signed and dated by the property owner, describing the existing roof and the proposed change
  • Photographs of the parts of the house the work will touch
  • The manufacturer's product sheet or a sample of the material, which the instructions name for roofs
  • Scaled plans, elevations or section drawings where they are needed to show the change
  • A list of the work to be done

You can email the application to hpc@historicflemington.com or hand in a paper copy at Borough Hall. A commission member then contacts you to check that it is complete.

Two of those items come straight out of a normal roof estimate. A roof inspection from us includes photos of what we find, and the product sheet for the shingle comes from its manufacturer, such as GAF or Owens Corning.

When does the commission meet, and how far ahead should you apply?

The commission meets once a month, on the third Wednesday, and a completed application has to arrive at least ten days before the meeting you want. The owner, or a representative the owner has authorized, must attend; the contractor and architect may come too.

The review page says there is no cost for a review. If your plans change after approval, the instructions allow an addendum application, as long as the original was approved within one year.

What roofing choices do Flemington's historic guidelines favor?

The guidelines favor keeping the roof's original look: repair first, and if something has to be replaced, match what was there. The roof chapter of the Borough of Flemington Historic District Guidelines encourages:

  • Replacing damaged or missing materials with new materials that match the original in material, size, shape, texture and color
  • Where the original material is no longer possible, using new material of similar size, shape, texture, pattern and color
  • Fasteners and flashings with an expected life span similar to the roofing material
  • Cleaning gutters and downspouts, typically every spring and fall, and checking the attic after a storm or a freeze

The same chapter discourages removing roof features such as chimneys, dormers, cupolas, weathervanes and finials; removing or altering drainage systems; adding rooftop features visible from a public way that change the roof's shape; putting on a roof in a style older or newer than the building; and covering or removing eaves and cornices. A photo caption in the guidelines adds that metal roofs need painting to limit rust.

When the commission reads an application, its review page lists the criteria it weighs, including the compatibility of existing and proposed materials and the relationship of roof shapes. The guidelines also say the commission offers advice on materials free of charge.

Does the historic review replace a construction permit?

No. The commission's review and a construction permit are separate steps, and the commission's role is only advisory.

Construction permits go through Flemington's Construction / Zoning Department, reachable at 908-782-8840, extension 220. The department's page says every inspection request has to be made through the SDL Portal. Ask the department whether your roof job needs a permit before work is scheduled.

Flemington historic district roofing: questions homeowners ask

Does repairing a roof in Flemington's historic district need a review?

Not when it is ordinary repair or maintenance in kind. Replacing a few damaged shingles with matching ones, or fixing flashing the way it was, needs no Historic Preservation Commission review. A repair that changes the material or the look of the roof does, so check with the borough first if you plan a different product.

Do you need a roof permit in NJ?

In Flemington, the historic review and a construction permit are two separate steps. The commission reviews the look of the roof; the borough's Construction / Zoning Department, at 908-782-8840, extension 220, handles construction permits. Ask the department about your job before work starts, and request inspections through the SDL Portal.

Are you allowed to redo your own roof?

Inside Flemington's historic district, the review rule applies to the work, whoever does it. If you change the roof's material or look, you still file an application with the Historic Preservation Commission, and the owner or an authorized representative must attend the meeting. Ask the Construction / Zoning Department about any permit.

Should a 20-year-old roof be replaced?

The roof's condition decides that more than its age. In Flemington's historic district this matters more, because a repair in kind skips the commission review while a replacement in new materials needs one. A free inspection with photos tells you whether a repair will hold before you plan a review.

The short answer

A roof replacement inside Flemington's historic district needs Historic Preservation Commission approval whenever it changes the roof's material or look, and the review is free.

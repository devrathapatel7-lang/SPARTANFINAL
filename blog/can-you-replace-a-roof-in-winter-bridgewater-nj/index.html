Winter Roof Replacement in Bridgewater, NJ

Yes. A roof replacement in Bridgewater, NJ can be done in winter on days warm and dry enough for the materials. Owens Corning treats anything below 40°F as cold-weather installation, with hand-sealed perimeter shingles, and NOAA normals show about 14 January days that reach 40°F at a nearby station. This guide answers one question for Bridgewater, NJ homeowners: whether a full roof replacement can happen between December and March, and what changes when it does. Spartan Roofing is a roofing company based in Somerset, NJ, and an Owens Corning Preferred Contractor. Our roof replacement page explains how a full replacement works, and our Bridgewater roofing page covers the township itself.

Can a roof be replaced in winter?

Yes, as long as the job waits for a stretch of workable weather. Owens Corning says a complete reroof in winter is possible "if you're willing to wait for a stretch of good weather," and warns that the project may take longer depending on how changeable the weather is.

The same Owens Corning article notes that every winter has its share of milder days, and that interior work such as attic jobs carries on in any season. A full tear-off is different, because the roof is open until the new layers are on, so it needs a forecast that holds.

How cold does winter get in Bridgewater for roofing work?

Cold enough that many winter days sit close to the 40°F line in Owens Corning's installation instructions. NOAA's 1991-2020 climate normals for its Somerville Somerset Airport weather station put the normal January high at 39.3°F and the normal January low at 20.6°F.

The averages still leave room to work. A normal January has about 14 days that reach 40°F, December about 20 and February about 17, according to the same NOAA station record.

Do shingles seal in cold weather?

Not on their own right away, which is why the instructions add steps below 40°F. Owens Corning says shingles put on in winter "may take longer to thermal seal and may even need additional hand sealing."

The installation instructions for Owens Corning's Duration shingles spell out what cold-weather installation means. Below 40°F, every shingle on the perimeter of the roof is hand-sealed with four 1-inch dabs of asphalt cement that meets ASTM D4586. Field shingles go on with a gap of no more than 1/16 inch at the end joints, so they don't buckle against each other when they expand in warmer weather. The instructions also call for extra care handling shingles when it is below 40°F.

Does the underlayment have a temperature limit too?

Yes. The ice and water barrier, a self-adhering membrane that goes under the shingles, has its own rule. Owens Corning says its WeatherLock Granulated ice and water barrier should be applied when the air and the roof deck are 40°F or warmer, and that below 40°F the underlayment "will not fully adhere until warmer temperatures."

The same instructions say to store the rolls at room temperature before a cold-weather install. A January job has to meet both the shingle rules and the membrane rule.

Is the Bridgewater permit any different in winter?

No, the season doesn't change it. Bridgewater Township's Code Enforcement Department lists roofing among the permits required only under specific conditions: for commercial properties, and for residential jobs where the roof sheathing is being replaced.

That second condition can come up partway through a job: if water has been getting in for a while, the decking under the old shingles can be soft, and we show you photos before anything extra is replaced. Our Bridgewater roofing page covers the township permit question in more detail.

Is winter a good time to schedule a roof replacement?

It can be, if your roof can hold out for a good stretch. Owens Corning notes that many contractors have flexible schedules at this time of year. A winter job can also get pushed back when the forecast changes.

If the roof is leaking now, the order changes: stop the water first, then plan the replacement. Our roof repair page explains how we trace a leak, and after storm or wind damage we can tarp a roof and document it for your insurance company.

Winter roof replacement in Bridgewater: questions homeowners ask

What time of year is the cheapest to replace a roof?

We don't publish prices. The cost comes from the roof itself, its size, pitch, the layers to tear off and the decking underneath, and the calendar isn't what sets it. You get a written price for your roof after a free inspection.

Should a 20-year-old roof be replaced before winter?

Going by age is a poor guide. A 20-year-old roof with sound shingles and flashing may only need a repair, while one with curling shingles or soft decking may need replacing. A free inspection with photos shows which one you have, and if a repair will hold, that is what we quote.

What if my roof starts leaking in the middle of winter?

Get the leak stopped first, with a repair or a tarp, and plan the replacement for a stretch of weather that allows a full tear-off. Water often gets in well away from the ceiling stain, so the repair has to go where it enters.

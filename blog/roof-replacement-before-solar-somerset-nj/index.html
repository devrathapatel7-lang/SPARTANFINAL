This guide covers one decision for Somerset, NJ homeowners: whether a roof replacement should come before solar panels, and what New Jersey's construction code says about a roof that already carries them. Somerset is part of Franklin Township, and our Somerset roofing page covers the town itself.

Should you replace your roof before installing solar panels?

Replace it first when the roof has fewer good years left than the panels will. The Department of Energy's homeowner guide to going solar says you "should also consider the age of your roof and how long until it will need replacement," and it notes that some rooftops are not suitable for solar because of age or tree cover.

A July 2021 article from the department's Solar Energy Technologies Office explains the timing. It says solar panels last about 25 to 30 years, "which is similar to the lifetime of a roof," and that doing both jobs at the same time avoids having the panels reinstalled after a later roof replacement.

How do you tell if a roof has enough life left for solar?

Look at the parts that fail first, because age alone is a poor guide. Our free inspection checks the shingles, the flashing, the decking where we can see it and the attic, and you get a photo report of what we found.

Curling shingles or soft decking point toward a replacement. A roof with sound shingles and flashing may only need a repair, and if a repair will hold, that is what we quote.

Can a roof be replaced with solar panels already on it?

Yes, but the panels come off first. New Jersey's Division of Codes and Standards says so in its Fall 2021 Construction Code Communicator: the panels "must be removed to install the roof covering."

The same article answers what happens when they go back on. Panels that are reinstalled after a reroof do not have to be redone to the newer roof access rules, because they are neither completely replaced nor newly installed.

Do you need a permit to replace a roof under solar panels in New Jersey?

For the roof covering itself, the state's answer is usually no on a detached house. The Division of Codes and Standards writes that a roof covering replacement on a detached one- or two-family dwelling is ordinary maintenance under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7(c)1x.

The solar wiring is a separate matter. If the rails of the solar system are removed and the grounding or bonding is compromised, the state says an electrical permit is needed so the system is reconnected properly. Homes in Somerset deal with the Franklin Township Construction Code Department, so ask it about your address before the work is booked, and ask again if soft decking turns up and has to be replaced.

What do New Jersey's solar access rules mean for the roof?

They keep open strips on the roof so firefighters can get across it. Section R324.6 of the residential code covers newly installed and completely replaced solar systems, and the state's Fall 2021 article lays out the main requirements:

RuleWhat it requires
PathwaysAt least two, on separate roof planes, running from the lowest roof edge, each at least 36 inches wide. At least one is on the street or driveway side.
Pathway on each array planeA 36-inch pathway from the lowest roof edge to the ridge, on the same plane as the panels, an adjacent plane, or straddling both.
Ridge setbackAt least 18 inches clear on both sides of a horizontal ridge when the panels cover 33% or less of the plan view roof area, and 36 inches when they cover more.
Escape openingsNo panels on the part of the roof below an emergency escape and rescue opening, with a 36-inch pathway to it.
ExceptionsDetached non-habitable structures such as garages, and roofs with a slope of 2 in 12 or less.

Source: New Jersey Department of Community Affairs, Construction Code Communicator, Fall 2021 (Volume 33, Number 3).

Those open strips are worth knowing about before a new array is planned. On a roof replaced first, the solar installer lays the panels out under these rules on fresh shingles, with no older roof sitting under them.

Does the direction and pitch of the roof matter?

Yes. The Department of Energy says solar panels typically perform best on south-facing roofs with a slope between 15 and 40 degrees, though other roofs may be suitable too. That is a question for the solar installer, and it is worth asking before you decide which slopes get new shingles first.

What to plan before a combined roof and solar job

The order of the work matters most. The 2021 Department of Energy article notes that roofers and solar installers often work in partnership, which helps when both jobs land on the same house.

  1. Get the roof inspected so you know whether it needs a repair, a replacement, or nothing yet.
  2. If it needs replacing, schedule the roof before the panels go on.
  3. If the panels are already up, agree with the solar installer or an electrician on who takes them off, who puts them back and who handles the electrical permit.
  4. Check with the Franklin Township construction office about permits for your address.

A full replacement from us carries our 25-year workmanship warranty, and the shingles carry the manufacturer's own warranty.

Roof replacement and solar in Somerset: questions homeowners ask

Can you replace a roof under existing solar panels?

Yes. The panels have to come off first, and New Jersey's Division of Codes and Standards says so directly. After the new roof covering is on, the panels go back on. If the rails were removed and the grounding or bonding was disturbed, an electrical permit covers the reconnection.

Should an older roof be replaced before solar?

Not on age alone. The Department of Energy tells homeowners to weigh the roof's age and how long until it needs replacing. A roof with sound shingles and flashing may only need a repair, while curling shingles or soft decking point to replacement. A free inspection with photos shows which you have.

How long do solar panels last compared with a roof?

The Department of Energy puts solar panel life at about 25 to 30 years and calls that similar to the lifetime of a roof. That overlap is why it suggests doing a roof replacement and a solar install together, so the panels don't have to come off for a reroof partway through their life.

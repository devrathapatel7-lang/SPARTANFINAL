Local Conditions

Common roofing challenges in Franklin, NJ

The problems we are called about most from Franklin Township are fallen limbs, moss and shade, older farmhouse framing, flat sections on outbuildings and additions, and wind across open ground. They differ from the dense Somerset section.

  • Fallen Limbs and Debris Storm winds drop branches onto roofs in the wooded parts of the township. A limb can crack decking without making a visible hole, so we check from the attic as well as the top.
  • Moss and Slow-Drying Shade Roofs that sit under mature trees stay damp. Moss lifts shingle edges and holds water against the surface, which wears the granules off early.
  • Older Farmhouse Framing Some houses in the township date back more than a century, like the Van Wickle House beside the canal. Roofs on older framing often need decking and rafter checks before new roofing goes on.
  • Outbuildings and Additions Barns, garages and rear additions add flat or low-slope sections. Membrane and flashing details there fail first, and we repair or replace them separately from the main roof.
  • Wind Across Open Ground Lots near fields and the Raritan and Millstone river corridors have little shelter. Wind lifts shingle tabs and ridge caps, and we look at fastener placement as well as the shingles themselves.

A call to the shop gets the inspection on the calendar, and the photo report shows you what is on your roof.

About the Area

All about Franklin, NJ

Franklin Township was incorporated on February 21, 1798, and covers 46.88 square miles in the Raritan Valley. It grew from 50,903 residents in 2000 to 68,364 in 2020, with housing units rising from 19,789 in 2000 to 24,426 in 2010. The township is farmland by history, and communities such as Franklin Park, East Franklin, Six Mile Run, Middlebush, Weston, Griggstown, Blackwells Mills, East Millstone and Pleasant Plains are all separate places with their own streets and lot sizes.

The Delaware and Raritan Canal, finished in the 1830s, runs along the township's northern and western edges and is now a 22-mile state park. The Millstone River passes Kingston, where the 1888 mill is the third on its site, and Ten Mile Run flows through a 684-acre greenway. Roofers think about water in these places.

Low ground by the canal and the rivers stays damp, and the trees that grow there drop leaves into every valley. We clear and check the valleys as we work.

More about roofing in Franklin

Some houses here are old. The Van Wickle House was built in 1722 and the Franklin Inn on Amwell Road around 1752, and farmhouses of that age have been re-roofed many times over.

If your house is one of them, expect the inspection to include a close look at the decking and rafters before we recommend a price. We tear off to the deck, replace the boards that are soft or split, and only then lay ice and water shield in the valleys and eaves, synthetic underlayment, drip edge and ridge ventilation.

Our owner handles the inspection and the explanation himself. He also tells owners when they do not need a full replacement.

A leak at a plumbing vent or a lifted run of flashing is a repair, and we say so. When a roof does need to come off, the full replacement carries our 25-year workmanship warranty, the shingles carry the manufacturer's warranty, and we finish with a magnetic nail sweep across the lawn and drive and a walkthrough with photos.

Frequently asked questions: roofing in Franklin, NJ

Do you cover all of Franklin Township, NJ, or just Somerset?

All of it. The Somerset section is where our shop is, but we also roof homes in Franklin Park, East Franklin, Six Mile Run, Middlebush, Griggstown, Blackwells Mills, East Millstone and the other communities in the township.

How do you handle roofs with heavy tree cover in Franklin Township?

We look at limb damage, moss, packed valleys and shaded slopes during the inspection on a wooded Franklin lot. On a replacement we use ice and water shield in the valleys and eaves, synthetic underlayment and ridge ventilation, which help a shaded roof dry out.

Do I need a permit to replace a roof in Franklin Township?

The Franklin Township construction office issues roofing permits. We handle the paperwork for you.

What happens after a storm drops a tree limb on my Franklin Township roof?

Call the shop. We tarp the Franklin roof, document the damage and work with your insurance company. The insurer decides what is covered, and we do not promise approval.

Local Conditions

Common roofing challenges in Bound Brook, NJ

Bound Brook roofs deal with river-valley storms, close neighbors and buildings of different ages. Five problems come up most often.

  • Storm damage from river-flood weather Floyd in 1999, Irene in 2011 and the April 2007 nor'easter all hit the borough. The wind and rain that come with those storms loosen shingles and flashing.
  • Flat and low-slope failures Flat roofs depend on their seams, fasteners and drains. When any of them opens, water sits and then leaks.
  • Valleys and walls on crowded blocks Where a roof meets a taller wall or a second story, the flashing takes a beating. We replace it instead of adding more sealant.
  • Attic moisture A damp attic rots the deck from underneath. We add ridge ventilation and check insulation baffles, so the deck dries out.
  • Gutter overflow between close-set houses With little room between houses, water that spills off a gutter lands on a wall or a neighbor's yard. We install seamless aluminum gutters sized to the roof.

About the Area

All about Bound Brook, NJ

Bound Brook sits along the Raritan River with Middle Brook and Green Brook forming its western and eastern edges. The borough covers about 1.7 square miles at roughly 43 feet above sea level, bordered by Bridgewater Township, South Bound Brook and Middlesex Borough. Route 28 runs east and west through the middle of town and US 22 clips the northern edge.

The town has a long record as a crossing point on the river. The Battle of Bound Brook took place on April 13, 1777, and a wooden bridge called Queen's Bridge crossed the Raritan from the 1760s. Downtown burned in 1881 and 1887, which led to the formation of the fire department, and the area was rebuilt.

The NJ Transit station building at 350 E. Main Street dates to 1913. For a homeowner, the point is that building ages are mixed, so we expect different decking, layers and flashing from one block to the next.

More about roofing in Bound Brook

Water has shaped Bound Brook more than anything else. The Raritan reached its highest recorded level in the borough during Hurricane Floyd in September 1999, and Main Street also flooded in 1938, 1955, 1973, 1996 and 2010. The Green Brook Flood Control Project, which ran from 1999 to 2015, was designed to protect the area from very large floods.

We do not repair flooded interiors. What we do is the roof above them: we check it after a storm, tarp it when needed and document the damage.

The 2020 count put the borough at about 12,000 residents in roughly 4,400 housing units. Dense blocks change how a roof job runs: a close neighbor means careful debris control, a clear plan for where trucks stand, and an honest talk about access before we start.

The borough construction office issues roofing permits. We confirm what your address requires and handle the paperwork.

On a replacement we tear off to the deck, replace damaged decking, and install ice and water shield, synthetic underlayment, drip edge, ridge ventilation and new flashing at every penetration. Full replacements carry our 25-year workmanship warranty.

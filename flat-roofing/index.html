How a flat roof fails in Somerset County, and how we fix it

A flat roof fails at a detail, such as a seam, a corner, a backed-out fastener or the joint with a wall, and we fix that detail or replace the membrane when the roof is past repair. A flat roof is never perfectly flat: it has a slight pitch, a drain or a scupper, and a membrane whose whole job is to keep water moving toward them.

When it stops working, water finds the weakest line it can: a seam, a corner, a fastener that backed out, the spot where the membrane meets a brick wall. On homes in Somerset, NJ these roofs sit over rear additions, kitchens, porches and garages, which is why the first sign is usually a brown stain on a ceiling and not anything you can see from the yard. We go up and look at the roof itself, because a flat roof cannot be judged from the ground.

More about flat roofing

Most flat and low-slope roofs we see are TPO, EPDM or modified bitumen. TPO is a white single-ply sheet whose seams are welded with hot air, and EPDM is black rubber, sold in large sheets and usually glued down.

Modified bitumen is a rolled, asphalt-based product, often finished with a granule surface. TPO reflects heat and shows up on newer work, EPDM has a long record on residential additions, and modified bitumen suits roofs with many corners and penetrations.

We tell you which one fits your roof and why, and we put the membrane type in writing on the estimate. You should never have to wonder what is being glued or welded to your house.

Almost every flat roof leak we trace in Somerset County starts at a detail, not in the middle of the field. Seams open where a sheet has been pulled tight through a cold winter. Flashing lifts along a wall.

A pipe boot cracks. A drain bowl fills with leaves and holds water against the membrane.

Fasteners back up under the sheet and raise a small bump that wears through. Because of that, we spend more time at edges, corners and penetrations than anywhere else on the roof, and a repair means fixing the detail itself, not smearing sealant across a wide patch and calling it done.

Central New Jersey gives a flat roof a hard calendar. Freeze and thaw works water into every small opening and widens it. Heavy snow from a nor'easter sits on a low-slope roof longer than it would on a steep one, then melts and refreezes at the drain.

Summer sun heats a dark membrane enough to make it move, and the movement shows up at seams and corners. Trees around the lots in Somerset County drop leaves and seed pods onto the roof and into the drains every fall. None of this is unusual, and all of it is manageable when the roof is checked once the leaves are down and once after winter.

Repair makes sense when the membrane is sound, the leak is at a known detail and the insulation underneath is dry. Replacement makes sense when the sheet is shrinking, the same area keeps leaking after patches, or the insulation is soaked and soft, and we show you photos before we recommend anything.

If the deck is damaged, it is replaced before the new membrane goes down. If a repair is all you need, we say so, and you do not pay for a roof you did not need.

Watch For These

Signs your Somerset County home needs flat roofing

  • Water stays on the roof after the rain has stopped
  • Brown ceiling stains under a porch, rear addition, kitchen or garage
  • Seams lifting, or the membrane pulling away from a wall
  • Bubbles, blisters or soft spots that give when you step on them
  • Cracks or splits in the surface, especially at corners and around pipes
  • Leaves and debris packed around drains and scuppers
  • A patched roof that keeps leaking in the same spot

FAQs

Flat roofing in Somerset County, NJ: frequently asked questions

Which flat roofing material is right for my home in Somerset, NJ?

TPO, EPDM rubber and modified bitumen are the common choices on low-slope roofs, and the right one depends on the size of the roof, how many corners and penetrations it has, how much foot traffic it sees and what the existing roof is made of. We explain the tradeoffs at the inspection and put the membrane type in writing on your estimate.

Why does my flat roof leak after storms in Somerset?

Storms push water to the weak points: open seams, loose flashing at a wall, cracked pipe boots and drains blocked with leaves, which let water pool against the membrane. A leak often shows up in a ceiling several feet from where the water gets in, so we trace it on the roof instead of treating the stain.

Does a flat roof replacement in Somerset need a permit?

Roofing work generally goes through the township construction office, which issues permits for projects in Somerset. We handle the paperwork, so you do not have to stand in line or fill out forms.

What warranty comes with a new flat roof from Spartan Roofing in Somerset County, NJ?

The membrane carries a manufacturer-backed material warranty. Workmanship terms are written into your estimate. Our 25-year workmanship warranty applies to full roof replacements, and we will tell you in writing how it applies to a flat roof before you sign anything.

Is the roof inspection really free for homes in Somerset County?

Yes, it is free and there is no pressure to book anything afterward. We use an on-roof assessment and send a photo report you can compare with other quotes.

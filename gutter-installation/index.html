The Difference

Why Somerset County homeowners choose Spartan Roofing for gutter installation

  • Formed to fit your house

    A gutter run is formed from a coil of aluminum at your house, one piece cut to the exact length of each eave. Corners and end caps are the only joints, and they are sealed.

  • Hangers and slope done properly

    Hidden hangers go in every 24 inches, and the run is set with a slight fall toward each downspout. A gutter that holds water in the middle sags and rots the fascia behind it. We set the slope before we fasten anything for good.

  • Drainage planned before we start

    Before the first cut we look at roof pitch, roof area and where the downspouts can drop. Oversized 3 by 4 inch downspouts take the water down, and splash blocks at the exits carry it away from the foundation.

How we install gutters on Somerset homes

A gutter job starts at the fascia, not at the gutter. The fascia is the board the gutter hangs from, and if it is soft or stained behind an old run, a new gutter will not hold on it for long.

Our crew looks at that board first on every Somerset County house and tells you what we find. When a section needs replacing, we replace the fascia board before the new gutter goes up, because fastening new aluminum to bad wood just moves the problem a few years down the road.

More about gutter installation

A recent job, from a Google review in March 2026: Justin Filimon had us replace his old gutters with new, bigger 6 inch gutters. Before the install we explained the problems with the old gutters and the whole process with the new ones.

We install K-style gutters, which have a flat back and a front profile that looks like crown molding from the street. Half-round gutters are also available for homes where that shape suits the architecture.

Five inch gutters are fine for most standard homes in Somerset County. Six inch gutters carry more water, and we recommend them on steep roofs and on houses with a large roof surface draining into one run. We size by the roof, not by habit.

Downspouts matter as much as the gutter. We use oversized 3 by 4 inch downspouts and place them where the water can leave the house instead of collecting beside it.

A splash block goes under each exit. If your yard slopes toward the foundation on one side, tell us at the estimate and we will plan the downspout on that corner around it.

Color is matched to your fascia and trim, so the gutters read as part of the house and not as an add-on. If you want to see the options in daylight against your own siding, ask at the visit.

If trees hang over your roofline, ask about micro-mesh gutter covers, an optional add-on that keeps leaves out of the trough. We quote the gutters with or without them and tell you whether your house needs them.

When the roof is also due for replacement, the order of work matters. The roof edge gets finished first, with drip edge and underlayment in place, and the gutters go on after that so the water coming off the shingles lands in the trough. We handle both, and a lot of Somerset County homeowners have us do roof and gutters together.

Old gutters come down and leave with us. Nails and screws that fall in the yard get picked up with a magnetic sweep, including along the driveway.

When the install is done, our owner walks the finished job with you and points out how the water will travel. If something is not right, you know who to call.

Watch For These

Signs your Somerset County home needs gutter installation

  • Gutters that sag, bend or pull away from the fascia along more than one side of the house
  • Joints that have opened up on sectional gutters, leaving stains down the siding
  • Rust, flaking paint or cracked aluminum on an old run
  • Fascia boards behind the gutter that are soft, dark or stained
  • Water spilling over the front edge in a normal rain because the gutter is too small for the roof
  • Downspouts that drop water beside the foundation and leave bare, washed-out soil

Our Gutter Installation Process

How gutter installation in Somerset County, NJ works with us

We assess your drainage, form the gutters on site, hang them on hidden hangers, then set the downspouts and walk the job with you. We do not put a clock on these steps, because the pace depends on your roofline and the weather that week.

  1. Step : 01

    Call, then drainage assessment

    Call (973) 938-2776 or send the form and we set a time. We look at roof pitch, roof area, the fascia and the downspout layout, and we walk the roofline with you. You get a written estimate with the profile and size we recommend and the reason for it.

  2. Step : 02

    Custom forming

    K-style or half-round aluminum is formed on site from a coil, in runs cut to the length of each eave. Five inch or six inch, depending on your roof.

  3. Step : 03

    Hidden hangers

    Heavy-duty hidden hangers go in every 24 inches, with the run pitched toward the downspouts. Old gutters come off before the new ones go up.

  4. Step : 04

    Downspouts and walkthrough

    Oversized downspouts are placed away from the foundation, with splash blocks set at the exits. Then we walk the job with you, sweep the yard for fasteners, haul away the old gutters and send you photos of the finished work.
